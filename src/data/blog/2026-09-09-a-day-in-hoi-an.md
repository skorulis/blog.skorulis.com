Hoi An was our first stop on our 2026 trip to Vietnam as we made our way north to Hanoi. We arrived late at night and after four meals on the flights did not even feel like dinner. Had a brief walk and then went to bed.

In the morning we found some cash and went straight to the Banh Mi Queen for breakfast. I am more used to eating them for lunch, but this one without chilli was a good fit. 150k VND (~$8 AUD) for breakfast and juice for the two of us.

Then we got a car to the Vegetable Village. I thought we could just wander around the streets, but it was a 35k VND (~$2 AUD) ticket which included a welcome drink. Very cute vegetable gardens, but there was not much to see.

After that we walked through the rice fields. It was a strange contrast to the busy streets of Hoi An to have this patch of farmland in the middle. Without any shade it was a hot walk. Seemed like everyone else was on a bike.

From there we walked to the coconut boat tour. One of those cases where it is a fun tourist activity that is very reasonably priced. 100k VND (~$5 AUD) got us an hour boating on the water with some extra activities like crab fishing and boat spins.

<img src="/images/hoi-an/coconut-boat.jpg" alt="Paddling a basket boat on the coconut boat tour near Hoi An" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From there we headed back to cool down and get some lunch. Went to a slightly more upscale joint and got some Hoi An chicken rice. The chilli jam it came with was amazing. 350k VND (~$19 AUD) for lunch.

From there we went for a walk around Ancient Town. It is also a ticketed area which includes entry to a number of attractions and museums.

By that time there was not much to do except find a place to relax with a beer. All the bars along the river in Hoi An have people out the front calling you in for happy hour. But one place with a 15k VND (~$0.80 AUD) fresh beer sign won me over. That let me have four for the price of my wife's one juice, which I did have. It must have been too good of a deal, because they took the sign down as another table ordered a round.

<img src="/images/hoi-an/cheap-beer.jpg" alt="A condensation-covered glass of fresh beer on a riverfront table in Hoi An" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

By then it was dinner time, where we found a cheap and authentic Vietnamese restaurant where I had cao lầu for the first time. The meat was great but I did not love the noodle texture. Really good value at only 120k VND (~$6 AUD) including a beer.

By then the lantern boats were out in force, though it seemed nicer to watch from the bank rather than be in one, no matter how many times we got asked. We went back for a walk through the old town to see it lit up at night and finished off the night at the 7 Bridges brewery. I got a double IPA there which cost nine times my previous beers at 140k VND (~$7.50 AUD), but it was delicious. Just cannot find cheap craft beers anywhere.

<img src="/images/hoi-an/lantern-boats.jpg" alt="Lantern boats crowded on the river in Hoi An at night" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

<img src="/images/hoi-an/expensive-beer.jpg" alt="A pint of double IPA at 7 Bridges Brewing Company in Hoi An" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The next morning we set out looking for breakfast, which required a bit of walking to understand what was on offer. Ended up having a different kind of banh mi which was more like an egg and bacon roll.

After that we finished off walking around An Hoi Island just to make sure we saw it all, and then got a transfer to Da Nang.
