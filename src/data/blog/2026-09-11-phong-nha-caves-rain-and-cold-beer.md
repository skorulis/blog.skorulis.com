On the morning of the 11th we headed to the Da Nang train station to get a 6-hour train to Dong Hoi and then on to Phong Nha. We left a day early after our cave tour plans got rearranged and then cancelled due to the incoming rain.

We had seats in the premium cabin, which was very comfortable except at the start when they played music featuring high-pitched singing at maximum volume. It was clear weather on the train to Dong Hoi, but then we hit an abrupt downpour during the hotel transfer which completely stopped by the time we arrived.

Phong Nha town feels like a tourist hub that serves as a base to get people out to the caves and onto tours. It is mostly just one street lined with restaurants and accommodation.

We walked up and down the main street of Phong Nha, all of which felt like a ghost town at the end of the tourist season. So we settled for a few drinks at Bee Pub and then got dinner across the road. Noodles again with a side of spring rolls and drinks for 235k VND (~$13 AUD).

The next morning we got picked up to do a trek and cave tour. It was raining heavily when we got picked up and that continued for the entire day. Luckily it is still 24°C and it is a hilly hike, so being constantly saturated is not cold.

<img src="/images/phong-nha/jungle-trek.jpg" alt="Hiking a muddy trail through lush jungle in the rain near Phong Nha" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

It was about 1.5 hours to the cave, which was gigantic and had a unique spiral-shaped roof caused by the swirl of the water. Unfortunately we did not get to do any of the swimming or river crossing as the rain had made that too dangerous. On the way back some parts of the track had turned into calf-deep pools of water.

<img src="/images/phong-nha/spiral-cave.jpg" alt="Spiral-shaped cave ceiling carved by swirling water above a sandy cave floor" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After we got back and cleaned off the grime from the hike we went out to find some food. With the rain now set in the town was even emptier and it was hard to find a place to eat. We ended up at the Palm Lodge and got rice and noodles again. Beers were only 20k VND ($1 AUD) so I opted for a second while waiting for the rain to slow. Total was 230k VND (~$12 AUD).

We slept well that night to the sound of rain and woke up to the river quite a bit higher. Had some eggs for breakfast at the hotel and then got a car out to Paradise Cave about 40 minutes away.

The cave system was very open with a wooden walkway running through it. Each cavern section was filled with stalactites and stalagmites of different types, running for about 500m. This cave is easily accessible via car, which made it a good choice for rainy weather.

<img src="/images/phong-nha/paradise-cave.jpg" alt="Large stalagmite formations and rimstone pools beside a wooden walkway in Paradise Cave" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

By the afternoon our street was ankle deep and the river was continuing to rise. Restaurants were open but everywhere seemed to be prepared for minor flooding. We ended up back at where we ate the first night due to so many places focusing on Western food. 230k VND (~$12 AUD) for lunch.

There was not anything else we could do at this point so I had a couple of beers on the second-floor balcony while the river edged upwards to cover the seating area below.

<img src="/images/phong-nha/flooding.jpg" alt="Floodwater covering the outdoor seating area at night with string lights reflecting on the water" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

By the morning the rain had slowed and the river level had peaked. We bought some cheap ponchos, hired bikes and rode down to the caving museum which had the history of the expeditions since 1990. Seems like each year they go out they find new cave systems.

Then we did a 10km bike ride to the "Pub with Cold Beer" in the rain. Not terribly pleasant but there was not much else to do. The highway section felt precarious as trucks and buses go by, but the side road was in surprisingly good condition.

<img src="/images/phong-nha/pub-with-cold-beer.jpg" alt="Holding a bike overhead under the Pub with Cold Beer sign while wearing a polka-dot rain poncho" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

It would be a nice place in the summer with the pool, but not so much in the rain. Only 2 other people made the trip. The menu has an option to pick a live chicken for lunch but I opted for pork noodles. 265k VND ($14 AUD) for lunch and 2 drinks each. The rain on the ride back was a little heavier but we made it through and dropped off the bikes. By that point we had done everything we could in Phong Nha so we sat and watched the river for a bit and then had our final dinner in the town, a chicken curry at Treehouse restaurant for 200k VND (~$11 AUD).
