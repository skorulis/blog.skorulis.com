On the morning of the 15th we got a transfer to Dong Hoi airport to head to Hanoi. Tickets were only $53 AUD each so it made more sense than spending a day on a train or bus. Dong Hoi airport looks like an industrial estate, they are in the process of expanding which is contributing to this, but the whole place just does not seem like a commuter airport.

After arriving in Hanoi we could not check in so went out to find some lunch. Picked a little place in the beer street while it was quiet and had my favourite banh mi of the trip while Amy had a pho for a total of 265k VND (~$14 AUD). Here we bumped into a Vietnamese-speaking Irish man named Alan and chatted with him for an hour over a few beers. It was a nice spot to sit but we figured we should check in.

It seemed time to do something slightly touristy so we went for a walk around Hoan Kiem Lake. There was less rain than down south but it was still pretty miserable.

There seemed to be a lot of places offering craft beer so we picked one and sat watching the street go by. 3 drinks set us back 285k VND (~$15 AUD).

<img src="/images/hanoi/hanoi-craft-beer.jpg" alt="Hanoi Craft Beer storefront on a rainy street with people under umbrellas crossing in front" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

When I looked more closely at others they all offer the same Hanoi Homebrew beers.

<img src="/images/hanoi/hanoi-homebrew.jpg" alt="Cheers with a frosted mug of amber beer and a Hanoi Homebrew apricot cider bottle on a Hanoi street" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The other thing I wanted to try was bia hoi (fresh beer), which was harder to find than expected. I thought there would be stores everywhere with people having a drink but we ended up at a quiet restaurant called Nha Hang Khanh Rau instead.

<img src="/images/hanoi/nha-hang-khanh-rau.jpg" alt="Nighttime storefront of Nha Hang Khanh Rau with glowing yellow signs and illuminated food photos" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The bia hoi tasted like beer-flavoured water but it was only 15k VND ($1 AUD). 2 fresh beers and a peach tea was only 90k VND (~$5 AUD).

<img src="/images/hanoi/bia-hoi.jpg" alt="A glass of pale bia hoi on a wooden table with blurred Hanoi street lights behind" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We walked for quite a bit afterwards through beer street with every shop calling us in for the best value beer. But most were at the 30k VND ($2 AUD) price, so maybe it is more of a tourist thing now. Seemed like the deeper we went the more expensive it got. We ended up finding a corner restaurant with tiny plastic chairs a little out of the main area and got pho and noodles with a beer for 190k VND (~$10 AUD).

In the morning we enjoyed our first buffet breakfast on the trip and then walked to the HK bus stop in the rain to get a bus to Sapa. Our original booking was for an 8:30 bus which got moved to 7:45 and we were told to be 30 minutes early. The bus did not show until 9am so we ended up standing around for almost 2 hours waiting. I could have really used that extra sleep.

The bus was quite fancy and we had to take our shoes off before getting on. The seats included a back massage mode in addition to the usual reclining. Most of the drive is pretty standard but it gets more beautiful and hairy as we headed into the mountains near Sapa and could see the terraced rice paddies until we were high enough to be surrounded by fog.

This stop only gave us a single day in Hanoi but we will be back later to visit it properly.
