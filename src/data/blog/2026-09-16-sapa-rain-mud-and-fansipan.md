We arrived in Sapa on the 16th at the end of a downpour. Being higher and further north meant it was a bit more miserable in the cold weather. The drop-off point was quite a way from our hotel (Sapa Hills Hotel). Luckily the bus company provided shuttle buses; unluckily these took forever to arrive. The whole trip ended up being 9 hours after all the waiting.

Sapa had a completely different feel to any of the other places in Vietnam. It is like a European mountain town, but still with scooters going in every direction. Because of the rain the whole town was covered in mist.

Since we got in a bit late we just had a bit of a walk around town before going out for a nicer dinner at Little Vietnam Restaurant. I got the beef which was served in a bamboo tube. Amy even tried the apple wine, which was actually made by soaking apples in spirits so it was quite strong but sweet from the apples. Dinner cost 650k VND (~$34 AUD) for both of us.

In the morning we did a 2km walk to Cat Cat Village. Entry was 150k VND (~$8 AUD). It was a fairly nice area to wander around despite the constant rain, which spoiled the numerous photo-stop locations. It's all paved walkways so at least your feet don't get covered in mud. There are a lot of shops trying to sell traditional wares, none of which interested me.

<img src="/images/sapa/cat-cat-village.jpg" alt="Rainy view of Cat Cat Village with a rustic log bridge over a waterfall and misty green mountains behind" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Other people had put on full traditional outfits to get photos by the river but probably would not be walking through the whole place like that. There was a little show in one spot as well.

After heading back to the hotel for a bit we went and got pizza for lunch. It came with a free beer but was quite bland. 250k VND (~$13 AUD) for one pizza to share. We did a little bit more wandering around the town and visited the museum, but the rain was getting quite irritating so we went back to the hotel to wait it out.

The break in the rain timed well with the night markets opening so we walked down to visit. We got some small pan-fried cake-like things but there was not much on offer so we walked back to Pho Bat Da, which translates to their signature dish: pho in a stone bowl. This turned out to be a self-assemble dish where the bowl comes out super hot with stock and then you add in all of the bits to cook. It is a fun experience but tastes the same as a regular beef pho. 200k VND (~$11 AUD) for both of us.

The next morning we went on a private tour to nearby villages. We were not really prepared for the level of mud walking through the rice paddies and our shoes ended up filthy. Luckily mine were already destined for the bin. At some point in the tour we picked up 2 extra guides who helped us not slip down in the mud in our inadequate shoes.

<img src="/images/sapa/muddy-shoes.jpg" alt="Top-down view of two pairs of sneakers completely caked in thick wet mud after walking through rice paddies" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

I was expecting rain but the day turned out mostly fine and started to warm up in the afternoon. We stopped for lunch at a local eatery in Lao Chai for a preset meal; the pineapple chicken was especially good. From there we walked for another 1.5 hours to Ta Van to wait for a taxi to take us back to the hotel. Due to a mix-up this was another hour wait.

For dinner we went to Little Sapa Restaurant and I tried out the wild boar and rice wine to mix things up. I quite liked the wine which seemed more like a spirit, but I would not order the boar again. Total cost was 407k VND (~$21 AUD).

In the morning we checked out and headed to the Fansipan cable car. The tickets cost 1050k VND (~$55 AUD) each including the train to the base of the cable car. It was pretty quick getting the tickets but after that it was constant lines at every point.

<img src="/images/sapa/fansipan-cable-car-queue.jpg" alt="Long covered queue of tourists waiting under a peaked pavilion for the Fansipan cable car" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Eventually we made it onto the train and the cable car. The cable car soars above the rice fields and then ascends into the clouds towards the top of the mountain.

Once at the top you can either walk up the 600 stairs or take multiple trains which requires more lining up. The walk up was nice since there were far fewer crowds, I would recommend it if you have the fitness since there's a number of extra photo stops.

<img src="/images/sapa/fansipan-buddha-stairs.jpg" alt="Steep stone staircase leading up to a large seated Buddha statue at the summit of Fansipan" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

As we walked the clouds moved higher and levels gradually became enveloped; we got really lucky with the timing. The very top was actually packed so we did not hang around long and walked back down. The whole place felt a bit Disneyland.

<img src="/images/sapa/fansipan-sea-of-clouds.jpg" alt="Traditional stone buildings with red tiled roofs on Fansipan perched above a sea of white clouds under a blue sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Once back in town we went to Cho Ban Pho for lunch. We had fried rice sitting on little plastic stools. Tasty and really good value at 155k VND (~$8 AUD).

Finishing up in Sapa my impression is that the place is loved to death. The little mountain town surrounded by local villages and rice paddies is being replaced by gigantic resort hotels with over 1000 new rooms currently under construction. It can be quiet during the day but int the afternoons the streets and shops are all crowded and traffic gets jammed in the one way streets.

We wanted to get the overnight train but it was fully booked so we got a HK bus back to Hanoi in the afternoon. This bus left on time and we got a 30-minute dinner break at a road stop where we shared a mystery-meat fried rice for 100k VND (~$5 AUD). Even though the bus left on time we still got in 1.5 hours late so we did not check in until after midnight. We had an early morning to catch the 8am train to Ninh Binh which is only 2 hours on the train.
