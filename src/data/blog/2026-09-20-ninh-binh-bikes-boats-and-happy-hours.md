## Day 1: Tam Coc and happy hour

We arrived in Ninh Binh on Sunday the 20th and stayed at Tam Coc Elegance House. It was 2000k VND (\~$105 AUD) for 3 nights, and we were able to check in early to a room with a nice view of the lake. We had moved our visit later due to heavy rain on our original timeline. We had clear days but the water level hadn't gone back down so many places were still closed either fully or partially.

We didn't have anything planned so we walked around the centre of town and found plenty of bars with cheap happy hour beers for later on. For lunch we went to Coffee Lan and sat by the lake watching the boats go by. Only 165k VND (\~$9 AUD) for 2 plates of noodles and a coffee.

We were going to go on a boat ride, but at 250k VND each (\~$13 AUD) it seemed like a lot for what it is, especially with the expectation of being sold to along the way. Instead we wandered some more. On the way back I grabbed a few beers to have on the balcony before we headed out for happy hour.

The happy hour plan was simple: look for places with a cheap beer sign and see how many I could get through. We ended up hitting 3 restaurants and having 3 beers at each. My beer cost for that night was under 100k VND (\~$5.50 AUD), but there weren't any cheap options for other drinks, so Amy's 3 drinks cost about 3x as much, which cancelled out all of my savings.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/happy-hour-draft-beer.jpg" alt="A glass of draft beer held up in front of a yellow happy hour sign advertising draft beer for 7,000 VND" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

- **Family Restaurant** had 7k VND (\~40c AUD) beers. The total was 101k VND (\~$5 AUD) for 3 beers and a cocktail.
- **Dao Phuket** was next, where we got fries, a cocktail and 3 beers for 175k VND (\~$9 AUD).
- **Pig Family** (yes, that is what they named the restaurant) was last. We got dinner along with 3 more beers and a cocktail for 243k VND (\~$13 AUD).

## Day 2: E-bikes, Bich Dong and Hang Mua

The next morning we hired e-bikes for 150k each (\~$8 AUD) to ride around. These weren't really necessary as the area is so flat, but the boost was nice for getting across busy roads. It also helped keep us cool.

First stop was Bich Dong, where we were confronted by some very grumpy parking officers who charged us 60k VND (\~$3 AUD) to park the bikes and enter. The path in was submerged but passable barefoot. It led up to a temple in a cave, which was pretty average overall. On the way out the parking attendant accosted us again and was trying to stop another tourist, who had already paid, from leaving. I'm pretty sure the whole parking thing is a scam and we should have just left the bikes further up the road.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/bich-dong-flooded-path.jpg" alt="People wading barefoot along a flooded stone path towards the ornate gate of Bich Dong pagoda at the base of a jungle-covered limestone cliff" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Next we tried to visit the nature park, but the main caves were closed so there wasn't any point paying the 250k VND (\~$13 AUD) ticket price. That also explained why the car park was completely empty.

We then followed a strangely clean, freshly paved road with no idea where it went. It ended at Den Voi, which was also flooded and empty. The road itself was really the highlight, with ducks and goats blocking our path.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/ebike-duck-road.jpg" alt="Amy wheeling an e-bike along a quiet paved road lined with trees, next to a flock of white ducks by a lakeside cafe" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We also tried Buffalo Cave, which was flooded too, and then had a beer and a coffee for 55k VND (\~$3 AUD) at Nhat Linh, watching the ducks wait to be fed.

Having exhausted the spots along the western road, we headed back into town for lunch. Once again we ate watching the boats on the lake, this time at Relax Coffee. 130k VND (\~$7 AUD) for banh mi, noodles and drinks.

After that we set back out to ride to Hang Mua. This ride wasn't as pleasant as the morning, since there was no shade and some of the trails turned out to be impassable. At the end the road was crammed with tour buses. This time I was ready for the scammers. They blocked the entrance and then swore at me when I said I would go to the ticket office first. Tickets were 150k VND (\~$8 AUD) each plus 5k VND (\~25c AUD) parking for each bike.

It was 483 steps to the top, which got very sweaty but wasn't a huge challenge. We never quite reached the summit because of a huge queue caused by some people who couldn't get the perfect Instagram shot and wouldn't leave. We still got the same views, then visited the second, lower peak and watched a house burning below us, close enough to hear the explosions and the fire engines arriving. No idea what the cause was.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/hang-mua-view.jpg" alt="View from Hang Mua over a jagged limestone peak with a stone staircase and small pagoda, looking out over flooded fields, a village and the city beyond" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After getting down absolutely drenched in sweat, we stopped at China Bar, which I had spotted on the way. They had 2 random Navy beers I had never heard of; this might be the only place they exist. Only 40k VND (\~$2 AUD) for 2 beers.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/china-bar-navy-beer.jpg" alt="A tall mug of dark beer with a thick foamy head on a wooden table beside a pond with lily pads" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After that we rode back to drop off the bikes, stopping on the way for Amy to buy a souvenir crab dish.

After washing off the sweat and sunscreen we went back to Dao Phuket for dinner. It came to 215k VND (\~$11 AUD), and the standout was a giant bowl of vegetable noodle soup for only 35k VND (\~$2 AUD).

## Day 3: Trang An

The next morning we hired regular bikes for 50k VND (\~$3 AUD) each and rode about 10km out to Trang An. It was mostly in the same direction as the day before, so we were getting pretty good at navigating the back streets. It cost 300k VND (\~$16 AUD) each for a 2.5-hour boat tour, which unfortunately didn't visit any of the caves due to the flooding and stopped at temples instead. It's a relaxing boat ride but a little boring, as it just goes up the river and back.

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/trang-an-boat-selfie.jpg" alt="Selfie of Alex and Amy in orange life jackets on a Trang An rowing boat, with a towering limestone cliff and other boats behind them" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We then headed back to town to have lunch by the water again, this time at Cozy Garden Restaurant for 175k VND (\~$9 AUD). The food was quite good and the service was great.

We had done enough riding, so we just took a short walk around the lake and then stopped for some craft beer tasting. 3 beers plus chips was only 180k VND (\~$9 AUD).

For dinner we went back to the same bar street, this time to Madame Mai to try the bun cha. This was one of my top meals of the trip; the meat and sauce were so good. The total was 213k VND (\~$11 AUD).

<img src="/images/ninh-binh/madame-mai-bun-cha.jpg" alt="A bowl of bun cha with grilled pork in broth, plates of rice vermicelli, a basket of fresh lettuce and herbs, and a bottle of Bia Viet" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />


Overall I really liked Ninh Binh and was sad to leave. Riding bikes around was a lot of fun, and Tam Coc was a good-sized town with plenty within walking distance.
