## Day 1: Getting there and free beer

On the 23rd we got a 9am van to Cat Ba. The drive took 4 hours to reach the ferry, and then another 1.5 hours for the ferry and transfer. The coastal road was nice, but we didn't pass anything worth coming back for.

We checked into the Bamboo House Hotel, which seemed like really good value at only 874k VND (\~$46 AUD) for 3 nights.

Walking out to look for food, the whole place felt like a ghost town. There are a bunch of new developments close to opening that are set to massively expand capacity, even though there already seem to be too many empty hotels. The design feels like it was modelled on a beachside town in the south of France. There is very little shade, and the heat is oppressive even in the late afternoon. At one point I thought there was a problem with Google Maps, since it kept showing me restaurants in the middle of the water. It turns out it just hasn't caught up with how much extra land they've built where the old pier used to be.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/sun-group-empty-street.jpg" alt="An empty paved street lined with brand new, unoccupied grey shophouses under strings of festoon lights and a clear blue sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We didn't find food, but we did find a sign promising free beer at The One Hostel. I thought there must be a catch, but we sat down and they kept bringing us beers until we left. They even gave us peanuts so we would want more beer. Needless to say, we would be back.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/free-tap-beer-sign.jpg" alt="A hand-painted chalkboard sign reading Welcome to The One, free local tap beer every day, 5:00pm to 7:00pm, with a drawing of a foaming beer mug" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We went to Yummy 1 for dinner and got a local speciality soup made with a paddy crab broth called bánh đa cua. It was a little fishy for me but nice to try something different. It was 220k VND (\~$12 AUD) including a mango sticky rice for dessert and a large beer.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/yummy-paddy-crab-soup.jpg" alt="A bowl of bánh đa cua with noodles and bean sprouts, served with fried dough sticks, a basket of fresh herbs and an iced drink" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

## Day 2: Lan Ha Bay cruise

In the morning we went back to Yummy 1 for breakfast, which cost 115k VND (\~$6 AUD) and included the biggest banh mi I've found. Then we headed off on a boat cruise around Lan Ha Bay. We had booked this the day earlier through Cat Ba Ventures for 1900k VND (\~$100 AUD). Since it's the low season there were only 14 people on a 50-person boat, which made it very roomy and relaxing. The surroundings are spectacular, with karst rocks jutting out of the water in every direction.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/lan-ha-bay-cruise-boats.jpg" alt="Calm green water in Lan Ha Bay with several large white cruise boats anchored between jungle-covered limestone karsts" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After a bit of motoring we stopped for an hour-long swim. The government says everyone needs to wear a life jacket, which makes it really hard to swim around, but we managed. The water was the perfect temperature.

After that we made another stop for lunch, which was mostly seafood. It was satisfying but not amazing. Then we stopped again to go kayaking. This was really fun as we got to paddle through water caves. One was jammed with kayak traffic and the other was so low we had to lie down to get through.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/lan-ha-bay-kayaks.jpg" alt="About a dozen people in yellow tandem kayaks holding paddles in the air on calm green water, with jungle-covered limestone cliffs behind them" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After that we went for another swim, during which I swam straight through a jellyfish. Luckily it only stung for 20 minutes, though the mark lasted until the next day.

We also visited a fish farm with some absolutely gigantic grouper and tuna, though they don't show up well in photos. Our guide went past each pen feeding them so they would swim up to the surface. From there the boat took us back to the wharf and a bus took us into town.

I went back to the free beer hostel and ran into the same German backpackers from the night before. They had told their Australian friends, who were as amazed by the deal as I was. The excitement and some card games got us drinking way more this time, which makes me question the strength of the beer, since I really wasn't drunk after probably 10 cups.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/free-beer-card-games.jpg" alt="Amy and a backpacker sitting at a wooden table covered in face-down playing cards and empty beer mugs at a bar with a wall of plants behind them" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We all went to get dinner back at Yummy 1 and then moved on to another hostel. I quickly bailed when I realised I was nowhere near drunk enough to fit in at that venue.

## Day 3: Beach walks and sunset

In the morning we went to Speciality Coffee for breakfast, which cost 220k VND (\~$12 AUD). It was more of a western-style breakfast, so it cost a little more than normal.

After breakfast we walked to Cat Co Beach 1 and then along the coast back into town. It wasn't that far, but it was so hot that I felt awful afterwards. We got lunch at Domik: banh mi, noodles, ice cream and a juice for 220k VND (\~$12 AUD).

We had a little break to wait for the temperature and UV to come down, and then went to the beach to wait for the sunset. We were originally going to rent chairs, but there wasn't much shade, so we walked up to the yet-to-open Sun Bavaria and sat in their garden by the beach. Since it was still a while until sunset, I went for a run along the beach, which inspired a few others to join in. It wasn't too bad while running, but afterwards I could not stop sweating.

<img src="/images/cat-ba/cat-co-beach-sunset.jpg" alt="The sun setting between dark karst islands over the water, with people silhouetted along the edge of a wide white sand beach" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

For dinner we tried out Yummy 2 (it was hard to move away from these places once we'd found an absolute winner), and I think they do a better banh mi. Both restaurants were just great value with a nice selection and everything we had was really good.

## Day 4: Back to Hanoi

In the morning we had a bus booked to Hanoi also through Cat Ba Ventures, so we just went back to Yummy 1, where I got what was essentially a BLT for 50k VND (\~$3 AUD), and then waited for the bus. The bus also made a stop so people could take the cable car instead of the boat which would have given a nice view.

Overall, Cat Ba was a kind of strange place in the low season. So much of it is closed during the day and only opens up after 5pm. It provides close access to Lan Ha Bay for cruises and has a huge national park, which I didn't get to visit. But the new Sun World construction is going to turn the main town into a giant beach resort.
