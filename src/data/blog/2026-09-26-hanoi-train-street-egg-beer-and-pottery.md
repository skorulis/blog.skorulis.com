## Day 1: Back from Cat Ba

On the 26th we took a bus from Cat Ba to Hanoi for our final stop in Vietnam. The first bus took us to the ferry and the next bus into Hanoi. It had one rest stop at a surprisingly fancy place which even had a free and clean toilet.

We got dropped off not far from the hostel but couldn't check in, so we went to find money and lunch. We've used the same TP Bank ATM 3 times in Hanoi now, since it's given the lowest fees with a flat 55k VND (~$3 AUD) fee below 5000k VND (~$263 AUD), while many are charging 4% of the transaction. This time we found out it wouldn't accept Amy's ING card for some reason.

We went to Hanoi After Dark for lunch since I saw a banh mi sign. They turned out to also have craft beer, so I grabbed one as well. The whole place seemed badly managed, with hidden menus, unavailable items and tables not cleared even though the place was empty. But I got a free beer upgrade, so that was nice. It was an absolute bargain for 2 banh mi and a beer, 95k VND (~$5 AUD) for lunch.

We stayed at Awesome Hanoi Hostel for 3 nights, costing 2100k VND (~$110 AUD). The place was tough to find because the front of it is a restaurant. The room was quite nice.

We walked down to Dong Xuan Market but didn't find anything that looked interesting, so it was just a bit of a wander. I thought I would check out Beer Street, which was just opening, but there wasn't anyone drinking outside of the pricey places, so I grabbed a few beers to have back in the hostel room, all of which were around 25k VND (~$1.30 AUD). I checked out the hostel rooftop, but since they don't allow outside drinks there wasn't anyone up there.

We then went to meet up with Amy's ex-work colleagues at Train Street. We were going to get a Grab there to be out of the heat, but it was going to take longer than just walking, so we did that instead. Train Street was buzzing with people on the tracks, so we found some seats upstairs and grabbed a drink. 10 minutes before the train came they cleared everyone off the tracks and it slowly moved through.

<img src="/images/hanoi/train-street-hanoi-beer.jpg" alt="A condensation-covered bottle of Hanoi beer held up above the crowded Train Street tracks at night" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After that we headed back to Beer Street and found a cheaper place where we had beer, soju and some dinner. I thought it would be easy to find a place to kick on next in Beer Street, but half of the bars were go-go clubs, and the one we ended up in was just completely dead even though they offered 2-for-1 cocktails. It was a bit of a late night.

## Day 2: French Quarter, Pasteur Street and a frozen beer

We got up late and Amy was under the weather. We went to a small restaurant for lunch where we stopped on our first Hanoi visit and got basically the same thing for 245k VND (~$13 AUD). We then walked into the French Quarter to look at expensive shops. It's such a different vibe to the chaos of the Old Quarter. I was starting to go downhill by this point, so I went back to the hostel for a rest.

We went out again and walked to Pasteur Street Brewing, since I enjoyed their beers in Ho Chi Minh. They were good here, but I only got to sample 2, a Pale Ale and an Amber Ale. Those perked me up a bit, so we headed to a night market for a wander. The great thing was I found an 18k VND (~$1 AUD) beer at the market so I could have a drink while Amy shopped. The beer was so cold it had frozen, which isn't great for the taste, but did keep my hands and the rest of me really cold while walking around.

## Day 3: Tay Ho, egg beer and rooftops

The next morning (our last full day) we started off heading to COC Legacy Specialty Coffee, where Amy got a fancy coconut coffee for 70k VND (~$4 AUD). It was a very elaborate process watching coffees be made. We then walked towards Tay Ho lake, stopping at various shops along the way. There was a very small island we could walk around as well, which for some reason had 4 separate outdoor exercise parks. Not sure why they need so many in one spot.

By the time we walked back to the hostel it was lunchtime, but we decided to try out an egg beer first at Cafe Giang. It tastes more like a spider than a beer, so it's an interesting experience.

<img src="/images/hanoi/cafe-giang-egg-coffee.jpg" alt="A Café Giang mug of creamy egg beer" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After that we wandered a lot of the Old Quarter looking in various shops, and then went and got out of the heat for a bit. By then it was beer o'clock, so we went and found a corner on the street surrounded by craft beer bars. I picked one with 80k VND (~$4 AUD) beers, which were ok. It was funny watching each bar expand their set of tables, and a lady selling fruit set up in the middle of the street.

<img src="/images/hanoi/beer-street-night.jpg" alt="Beer Street at dusk with neon Hanoi craft beer signs, street vendors and people sitting at tables in the road" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From where we were sitting we could see some really cute rooftop restaurants, so we went to Hello Hà Nội and sat overlooking the chaos below. This was a little more expensive at 400k VND (~$22 AUD).

After dinner Amy went back to the hostel and I caught up with an ex-work colleague for a few beers.

## Day 4: Pottery, sausages and a long way home

The next morning was our last day in Vietnam, but we didn't really have much to do until our 9pm flight, so I slept in while Amy any when shopping and came back with more pottery.

<img src="/images/hanoi/pottery-shop.jpg" alt="Amy browsing a ceramics shop with floor-to-ceiling shelves of mugs and tables covered in patterned plates" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We then checked out and got a small lunch at Khanh Rau Beer. I got to try the last dish I've been looking for, which was deep-fried fermented pork sausages. Very moreish beer food for 75k VND (~$4 AUD).

There weren't any cheap direct flight options, so we had an 8-hour layover in Kuala Lumpur. It's a pretty crappy airport to stop in, where the regular seats are designed to stop you from lying down and there are massage chairs everywhere. The wifi is spotty and there weren't good places to charge my phone. We spent most of the time in the cinema area watching terrible movies which frequently buffered, because at least they have better chairs. Eventually we boarded and got through the next 9-hour flight and got back home.
