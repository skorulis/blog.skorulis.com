## Day 1: Landing on no sleep

We landed in Osaka at 7am on the 13th of October running on almost no sleep. We got an early check-in for the Airbnb, but that still left us with 4 hours to use up after taking the train into central Osaka. We spent some time trying to figure out how all the underground stations connected and what floor was what. We spent a few hours wandering around dragging our luggage before we could check in, and then after a refreshing shower went back outside and wandered around luggage-free, which was much more enjoyable. We didn't go anywhere specific, just checked out what was within walking distance.

We were both pretty wrecked from the flight, so we went to the supermarket and grabbed a bunch of pre-made meals, sashimi, drinks and breakfast for the next morning for ¥3800 (~$35 AUD). The sashimi was tuna offcuts, which made it really good value. I don't mind that it's not all uniform.

## Day 2: Osaka Castle, Umeda and the bottom rung of Japanese whisky

In the morning, after a solid 12 hours of sleep, we went to Osaka Castle. We did a full lap walking through the surrounding parks and around the fortifications. It's an absolutely gigantic area with massive stones used for the fortifications and foundations. The leaves were starting to turn in the plum garden, which made for some really pretty views.

<img src="/images/osaka/osaka-castle-stone-walls.jpg" alt="Looking up at the corner of Osaka Castle's white keep and gold trim from the base of its massive stone foundation wall" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From there we went to Osaka Station, as we needed to pick up some rail tickets. That turned into quite a long process when the automated machine refused to accept the credit card used for the booking. After about 20 minutes of waiting for a human we got it all sorted, ready to catch the bullet train to Hiroshima later in the week.

We didn't have any lunch plans, but walked into the B2 level of Lucua and found a Japanese food court. We found some delicious-looking fried bento boxes with rice, grabbed a beer from Family Mart and sat outside near Umeda Station for lunch. All of it came to around ¥1900 (~$17 AUD), which was a great way to do lunch.

From there we walked all the way back to Namba, which was about a 5km trek. It was interesting to see the city go from upmarket to the seedier areas around Shinsaibashi and then back through Dotonbori. One minute it's all farmers markets and boutique stalls and suddenly it's bars and strip clubs. Along the way I was looking for places where I could buy a nice bottle of whisky to take home, but ended up finding Akashi Red for only ¥800 (~$7 AUD) and figured I should try out the bottom rung of Japanese whisky.

On the way home we grabbed some more food for dinner from Family Mart. I tried out my whisky, which was about as bad as you would expect for the price, but it tasted ok mixed with iced tea.

<img src="/images/osaka/akashi-red-whisky.jpg" alt="A bottle of Akashi Red blended whisky next to a frosted glass of whisky on ice on a small wooden table in the Airbnb" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Just across the road from our stay was Osaka Namba Brewery, which only opens at 8pm. I kept checking out the window, as there was no indication that would actually happen, but then bang on 8pm someone showed up and people started going in. They named all their beers after songs and had old rock concerts playing on a screen. I had 2 beers there for ¥650 (~$6 AUD) each for a half pint and then called it a night.

## Day 3: Himeji Castle and Mt Shosha

We got up early in the morning to catch the train to Himeji. It took about an hour from Osaka and cost ¥1460 (~$13 AUD). The centre of Himeji felt very quiet compared to Osaka. We walked to the castle and paid the ¥2600 (~$24 AUD) entry ticket cost each. I'm glad we got there early, because it was already quite busy. It took about 2 hours to fully walk through the castle and the surrounds. The whole interior is wooden with narrow stairs between each level. You take your shoes off before entering, and the castle is dimly lit, which together creates an interesting experience. Overall I think I preferred walking around Osaka Castle which had a more grandiose feel. There was also a garden section that came as a combo. It was a nice place, and some of the names of the sections, like "The garden with a hill and pond", were funny.

<img src="/images/osaka/himeji-castle-moat.jpg" alt="Himeji Castle's white keep rising above the trees, with a wooden bridge over the moat reflected in the water in the foreground" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

For lunch we went to Obanzai Restaurant Osaiya in Himeji, which looked like a house from the outside except for an open sign. The place served a dish of the day, which was grilled salmon on Thursday, along with 3 side dishes plus rice and miso. It was like sitting at a bench in someone's kitchen, with only 8 seats. Everything was delicious and a bargain at only ¥990 (~$9 AUD) each.

After lunch we caught a bus to the bottom of Mt Shosha. There is a cable car to take you up, but we hiked it instead. There's a whole Buddhist temple complex called Engyō-ji at the top, which costs ¥800 (~$7 AUD) to enter. It's got some really ancient-looking wooden temples. The walk was about a 7km round trip and took 2 hours.

We caught the bus back from Mt Shosha to Himeji Station and did a brief explore, which led us to buy 18 mini dough balls for ¥500 (~$5 AUD) before getting the train back to Osaka. They made for a good snack but were a bit plain. Once again we went to the supermarket for dinner, this time getting a bunch of sashimi along with some pork buns from 551 Horai.

## Day 4: Hoshi no Buranko, puffer fish and a big night

In the morning we took 3 trains to get to Kisaichi so we could walk to the Hoshi no Buranko bridge in Hoshida Park. The loop we first tried to take was blocked, so we had to go a different way, which was more through the town. It ended up being a 10km round trip and a pretty easy walk overall. The bridge view is nice, but otherwise there's not that much to see.

<img src="/images/osaka/hoshi-no-buranko-bridge.jpg" alt="The Hoshi no Buranko suspension bridge stretching across a forested valley, framed by a heart-shaped archway covered in leaves" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

After getting back we headed to a little sushi restaurant where you ordered pieces via a QR code. We learned that puffer fish has no flavour and is very chewy, and that eel liver is pretty good. With drinks it cost ¥3700 (~$34 AUD). From there we tried to go to an arcade bar, which was packed, so we went next door to Elephant Bar since they had an N64 out the front. I had some snake whisky, which was quite sweet and easy drinking.

<img src="/images/osaka/habu-snake-whisky-shot.jpg" alt="A small shot of habu whisky held up in front of large glass jars of snake-infused liquor labelled Habu Shot, Habu Whisky and Mamushi Shot" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Then we went to a standing craft beer bar, which had some delicious options, and met some Dutch and Americans who continued with us for the rest of the night. We visited many bars including an Australian bar which had Australian beers but very little vibe and Beer Bak Stop where I had more craft beers that I can't remember. I drank way too much and we didn't get home until 2am.

## Day 5: Kobe and kushikatsu

On the 17th we got up a little late and got the train to Kobe. We didn't really have a plan, just walked to the harbour and around the arcade shopping mall. I really do enjoy the partially outdoor layouts they use here for shopping malls. The harbour felt a bit bland, but it didn't help that it was very hot and there's almost no shade. The best recommendation I found for beef was at Steakland and that just felt wrong so we moved on.

Later that night we went to Shinsekai and got an assortment of deep-fried items on skewers for dinner, called kushikatsu, costing ¥3200 (~$29 AUD). Worth the experience, but it's not a food that calls me back. I went back to Namba Brewery across the road so I could try a few more beers and then called it a night.

## Day 6: Shinkansen to Hiroshima

In the morning we checked out and headed to Shin-Osaka to catch our first Shinkansen bullet train to Hiroshima. In the station I got a self-heating bento box and a beer for the train for ¥1900 (~$17 AUD). And then we were off, rocketing towards Hiroshima. The train was quite comfortable, making it a great way to travel.

Overall I really liked Osaka. It was a slightly toned-down version of Tokyo, with lots of little streets to explore where each one had something different.
