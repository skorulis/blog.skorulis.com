## Day 1: Arrival and the Peace Park

We arrived in Hiroshima on Sunday the 18th. We timed this one better, so we didn't have too much time to kill before check-in and used some of it to book tickets back to Osaka. We checked in and then started looking for what to do, but the Wi-Fi was down, so we just went for a walk around the Peace Park area. It's a nice park with a peaceful atmosphere and the A-Bomb Dome is a reminder of the past. We grabbed some supermarket okonomiyaki for dinner, which ended up being the only meal of the trip that I haven't enjoyed. I guess I shouldn't have taken the big one.

## Day 2: Miyajima and Mt Misen

The next day we went to Miyajima Island. It required a tram, a train and a ferry, the latter two of which were free with our JR Pass. The island is a little village and a giant mountain to climb. The climb took us about an hour to do the 2.5km track, since it's stairs most of the way going up 500m. There are some great views from the top, but the rain came in and obscured them. The whole walk is beautifully maintained, with multiple small shrines along the way.

<img src="/images/hiroshima/miyajima-mt-misen-view.jpg" alt="View from the Mt Misen trail on Miyajima looking out over forested hills and a bright yellow ginkgo tree to the islands of the Seto Inland Sea fading into mist" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Back in town there are so many large shrines that I got over them. Each has something different, but after a point it all starts to blur together. There's the famous torii gate in the water which is an iconic location but it's not that exciting to actually see.

The shopping street had an unexpected Miyajima brewery, where I grabbed a tasting paddle for ¥1000 (~$9 AUD), and many stores selling oysters prepared in different ways. It was about lunchtime, but it was hard to find a substantial meal. Amy purchased a bunch of maple-leaf-shaped cakes called momiji manju. I had one with a cheese cake filling which was really nice. I finally found a beer vending machine that I could use and grabbed one for the walk as a deer wandered behind me. I knew the island had deer but didn't expect them to be wandering down the main streets. They're indifferent to people unless it looks like you have something they can eat.

It was 3pm by this point, so we started the journey back to Hiroshima. I think we overexerted ourselves on the hike and couldn't do much after getting back except have a supermarket dinner. Miyajima is definitely worth making time for.

## Day 3: Hiroshima Castle, Saijo sake street and Okonomimura

The next day we first stopped in at Hiroshima Castle, which was nice to walk through but felt underwhelming after seeing Osaka and Himeji castles. It was free to walk through and on our way, so there wasn't any reason not to. We skipped the gardens next door.

<img src="/images/hiroshima/hiroshima-castle-moat.jpg" alt="The reconstructed gate and turret of Hiroshima Castle reflected in the still moat, with modern apartment towers behind under an overcast sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From there we went to Saijo to visit sake street. We visited six breweries: Saijotsuru, Kamotsuru, Fukubijin, Kamoizumi, Kirei and Sanyotsuru. Each one was a little different. We spent ¥2300 (~$21 AUD) having a tasting or two at each place. My favourite was Kamotsuru, which explained what defines high-quality sake and had lots of tasting options. All the breweries were good, but they lacked some variety in styles, so visiting them all isn't necessary.

<img src="/images/hiroshima/saijo-sake-tasting.jpg" alt="A three sake tasting set in small plastic cups on a white tray at Kamotsuru, in front of bottles of daiginjo sake with gold wrapping and tassels" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The mascot for the town is a mischievous raccoon dog who sometimes gets drunk, which matches the atmosphere. We were told that over the Saijo Sake Festival in early October there were 230k visitors, but today it would have been under 1000, which made for a very relaxing day where we could easily get served or ask questions. Since it was quiet there weren't many restaurants open, so we just got snacks from Lawson to get us through until dinner.

By 3pm we were a little tipsy and got the train back to Hiroshima. On the walk back we detoured via Hijiyama Park, which gives full views of the city. Overall, though, Hiroshima isn't a city that gives great views; it's a lot of concrete apartment blocks.

For dinner we went to Okonomimura, which is three levels of okonomiyaki restaurants where they make it in front of you. The place was packed out as a school group came in, but we managed to find a seat. They take all the food and drink orders until the space around the grill is full and then make them all at the same time. It took about 30 minutes to get the layers put together, and then you eat it directly off the grill with a little spatula. It's a fun experience and a big meal, but there's a fishy taste that I don't like. Maybe this style of okonomiyaki isn't for me. It cost ¥4200 (~$38 AUD) for dinner and a few beers.

<img src="/images/hiroshima/okonomimura-grill.jpg" alt="Rows of Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki being built on a long steel grill at Okonomimura, with piles of noodles and cabbage topped with thin crepes" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

## Day 4: Peace Memorial Museum and Hondori

The next day was supposed to be raining, so we planned for the Peace Memorial Museum in the morning. We got there at 9:30 and there was no queue to get in, but it was still very crowded inside and slow to move through. I was glad to get there early, as the slow-moving queue just keeps extending further back. It's got a lot of relics from buildings and stories from those who were in the blast.

Luckily it still hadn't started raining, so we headed to the Hondori covered arcade shopping area so we would be sheltered when it did. There's a lot going on, but I couldn't find anything I wanted. For lunch we went to an onigiri restaurant and got two each for ¥1350 (~$12 AUD). Quite filling and tasty. We did a little more shopping, and I got myself a new pair of Converse for ¥5900 (~$54 AUD) to replace the ones I got in Vietnam, where the soles were starting to come off. I grabbed a few beers to have back at the apartment while we packed to head off the next day.

## Day 5: Shinkansen back to Osaka and on to Nara

We caught the Shinkansen back to Osaka at lunchtime, and I had myself a luxurious supermarket sushi meal on the train. From Osaka we caught the JR line to Nara, which takes longer than you would expect since it takes a wide loop around Osaka.

<img src="/images/hiroshima/shinkansen-supermarket-sushi.jpg" alt="A supermarket lunch on the Shinkansen tray table: seared bonito tataki, salmon nigiri and inari sushi in plastic trays" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Overall, Hiroshima reminded me of Canberra in Australia. It's a nice place with wide streets and it's clean, but it feels like there's not that much there outside of the Peace Park inside Hiroshima City.
