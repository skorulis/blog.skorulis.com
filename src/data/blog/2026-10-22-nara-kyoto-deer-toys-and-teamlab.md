## Day 1: Arrival in Nara

We arrived in Nara on the 22nd, perfectly timed to check in. This place was really nice and even had a separate living area. The bedroom is very Japanese, with tatami mats, mattresses on the floor and sliding screen doors for privacy.

I decided to go for a run and did 7km, randomly heading through the city towards the park. It was a good way to find some places we could return to the next day. We then walked to The Big Extra supermarket and stocked up on food. It was odd to see a large supermarket like this in Japan, with a big car park out front.

## Day 2: Naramachi, the deer park and Todai-ji

The next day we walked into the Naramachi old town and just kind of wandered. Along the way we found a few places to stop in, all of which had free admission. But mostly it was just wandering through the narrow streets taking everything in.

Our first stop was the toy museum. This place was really cute and had tables set up with old toys you could play with. They were surprisingly ingenious designs that took a while to figure out. It might have been made for children, but I enjoyed myself.

<img src="/images/nara/naramachi-toy-museum.jpg" alt="Amy trying out traditional wooden puzzle toys laid out on a low table in a tatami room at the Naramachi toy museum, with a wooden staircase chest against the wall" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Next was the Historical Materials Preservation House. This one was a more traditional museum with old documents and maps. Small and not very exciting. Then a traditional house and a lattice house, which were nice for seeing the older design patterns. Both places had an outdoor kitchen, which I wouldn't have expected. We briefly visited Goryo Shrine, but it seemed like there was an event or ceremony in progress and we didn't want to intrude.

We then headed north into the deer park. I expected a fenced-in area with deer like a zoo, but it's a public park where huge numbers of deer live and then spread out into the surrounding streets. The place is absolutely packed with deer. We bought some shika senbei crackers for ¥200 (~$2 AUD) to feed them. Some of the deer are polite, while others will nip at you if you hold out on feeding them. If you show them your empty hands, they will usually move on to the next mark.

<img src="/images/nara/nara-park-deer-feeding.jpg" alt="Amy feeding a shika senbei cracker to two young deer on a gravel path in Nara Park, with more deer resting under the trees in the background" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The main areas of the park are packed with tourists, who I think miss out on the charm of the old town, which was basically empty.

The Nandaimon Gate in front of Todai-ji temple is absolutely massive and supported by the trunks of entire trees. We couldn't get inside the actual temple due to the size of the line, but we got a good look through the fence. The temple complex is also full of deer, even though feeding is discouraged there.

<img src="/images/nara/todaiji-nandaimon-gate.jpg" alt="Looking up at the huge two-storey wooden Nandaimon Gate at Todai-ji, with weathered red timber brackets and massive tree-trunk pillars above a crowd of tourists, school kids in yellow hats and a few deer on the stone forecourt" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

For lunch we walked down Mochiidono shopping street and went to Pinocchio for some omelette rice. It was quite satisfying and savoury, but it's not going to be one of my staples. Lunch was ¥2150 (~$20 AUD) for the two of us. It was nice to sit away from the crowds for a bit.

Afterwards we wandered through the shopping area for a bit. I grabbed some craft beers at Liquor Mountain and then walked to Naramachi Brewery and sampled two beers (an IPA and a spiced ale) before heading back to the apartment. While the place had a lot of space, it lacked a washing machine, so we had to spend an hour sitting at a laundromat. Dinner was once again from the supermarket, where I also got a bottle of nigori (cloudy) sake so I could sample sake outside of the boutiques. It was pretty good, and I'm getting better at understanding all the ways sake is labelled.

## Day 3: Kyoto in the rain

The next morning the rain from the incoming typhoon had started. We got the train to Kyoto, got off at Shijo and made our first stop at Nishiki Market. It seemed like a lot of the options were premium foods aimed at tourists. Amy got a Snoopy yaki, which was cute but tasted a bit plain. Next to the market is Teramachi Street, which has a better mix of shops, with more space and fewer people. There's also a giant animatronic crab at one end.

<img src="/images/nara/teramachi-giant-crab.jpg" alt="Looking down the covered Teramachi shopping arcade on a rainy day towards a giant red animatronic crab mounted on the front of the Kani Doraku restaurant" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

At this point it was lunchtime, so we walked down Pontocho Alley, but there wasn't anything that suited us. We ended up at a gyudon restaurant chain called Sukiya, which was really good value at ¥1500 (~$14 AUD) for both of us. It's thinly sliced cooked beef on top of rice, and it even came with a salad to get some veggies in.

After lunch we visited Hanamikoji Street and a bunch of shrines and temples: Kennin-ji, Kodai-ji and Yasaka Shrine, with the last being my favourite. All of the areas were busy, but not overwhelmingly so. The park behind Yasaka Shrine also has a large cemetery that extends up the side of the hill, which looks really cool.

That took us into the afternoon, and we had done enough to call it a day. We went back to Nara, got changed and then headed out to find dinner. First we went to Yamato Craft Beer bar, where I had two really good beers (Yamato Breeze IPA and Shima Saison) with snacks for ¥3000 (~$27 AUD). After that we walked to an Osaka Ohsho restaurant since we felt like gyoza, which turned out to be very good. We got two plates of gyoza, fried chicken and a beer, which might not have been enough to fill us up, but the lovely couple next to us gave us an extra plate of gyoza they couldn't finish. It was all great and only cost ¥1600 (~$15 AUD) for dinner. That was us done for the day, so we headed home, where I had a few craft beers already waiting in the fridge. A quiet Saturday night.

## Day 4: teamLab and Kyoto craft beer

In the morning we went back to Kyoto, starting off at the teamLab Biovortext exhibit since we liked the one in Tokyo. Tickets were ¥4800 (~$44 AUD) each and we spent about 2.5 hours walking through. There are a lot of separate rooms, each showing its own thing. Some are vivid digital graphics and others are tactile or sculptural. I think my favourite was the room filled with soapy foam bubbles which floated through like clouds. The place is designed with no set route, so it feels like an exploration, but at times that means trying to figure out if you've found all the exhibits. In that regard I preferred the Tokyo version with a defined path. Definitely worth the visit.

<img src="/images/nara/teamlab-kyoto.jpg" alt="Amy reaching out to touch one of dozens of glowing egg-shaped glass sculptures swirling with pink and green light in a mirrored room at teamLab Kyoto" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From there we walked to Kyoto Beer Lab and Takashima Breweries to have a few craft beers, though I didn't find anything that exciting. Surprisingly, that used up the day, so we headed back to Nara.

## Day 5: A lazy typhoon day

The next day a storm from the Typhoon came through, bringing a huge amount of rain, so we took the opportunity to have a lazy day. We only ventured out to get lunch at Osaka Ohsho again, and my legs were already saturated by the time we got there. This time I ordered gyoza and dan dan noodles, which ended up being quite a large lunch and cost ¥3000 (~$27 AUD) for both of us. I was planning to buy some whisky to take home, but it didn't seem like a good idea in the rain. So instead we just grabbed what we needed for dinner and breakfast and headed home.

## Day 6: Leaving Nara

On the 27th we walked back to Nara Station to catch a bus to Nagoya. On the way we passed two deer who had wandered several kilometres from the park and were now holding up traffic. Bus tickets were ¥3000 (~$27 AUD) each taking 3.5 hours. There wasn't any point paying double for a fast train, as we would need to wait to check in anyway.

Overall, Nara is a destination built around the deer park, but the old town is great too. Past that, there really isn't much to do. For Kyoto, I can see the appeal as a one-stop destination in Japan to get your cultural fix outside of Tokyo, but when doing a longer trip I prefer the smaller, less crowded attractions.
