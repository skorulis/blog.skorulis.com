## Day 1: Arrival and a night in a hostel

We arrived at Matsumoto Station at 2pm on Saturday the 31st of October. It was really hard to find accommodation for the Saturday night, so we were booked in for one night at the Matsumoto Castle Hostel. I've not stayed in a Japanese hostel before. It was much cleaner than other hostels I've been to, but it didn't have much of a social side since everyone was being quiet and respectful.

After we got checked in we went to the Matsumoto Brewery location near the shopping street. The bottom floor is standing bar style, which let us meet some regulars and an American teacher living in Japan. The locals laughed at us when we said we were spending 4 nights in Matsumoto. I had two beers there before we headed to a British pub called Old Rock for dinner. Oddly, this pub was organised as a restaurant. I got to try the local Sanzoku-yaki fried chicken. Good pub food with a ginger hit.

We walked around town, but most places were restaurants and very full, so we grabbed some snacks and sat in the park. There were lots of groups of girls videoing themselves doing dances in Halloween costumes. There was a costume party happening under our hostel, but the ¥3500 (~$32 AUD) entry seemed very steep for a party in an emptied-out pizza restaurant. We went back to the hostel and crashed in our little cubby hole beds.

## Day 2: Castle grounds, a ridge walk and Frog Street

In the morning we checked out and dropped our bags at the Welcome Hotel. We went to Paneterie Maarunaka and got a collection of pastries for breakfast, ¥1500 (~$14 AUD) with a coffee. Many of the places in town didn't open until 11 or 12.

We started our day's exploration at Matsumoto Castle, but decided to just do the outside while it was sunny, since the inside was very busy and we didn't have much planned for the next day. It was a good time to get some exterior photos. The black paint makes it look quite imposing. There was a chrysanthemum exhibit with some amazing bonsai versions. There were also some local craft markets nearby, which were nice for a wander.

<img src="/images/matsumoto/matsumoto-castle.jpg" alt="Matsumoto Castle's black and white six-storey keep sitting on its stone walls, reflected in the still water of the moat under a blue sky with a pine tree to the right" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Our next stop was Shiroyama Park, about a 30 minute walk away. There is an observation tower there which lets you see all the surrounding mountains and even out to the snow-capped peaks where the ski resorts are.

We took the ridge walk from there to Matsumoto Alps Park. I was hoping for something more precarious, but it's just a slightly uphill walk through the forest to the other park.

This park is much bigger and has a free museum with many taxidermy animals, butterflies and beetles. As we walked past the museum area we came across the main part of the park, which was filled with families having picnics. There was a free zoo with monkeys and raccoon dogs, a metal toboggan run that I couldn't see the bottom of which pulls you back to the top, a climbing mountain with a giant slide and multiple playgrounds. I could see why a family would come here on a sunny day like today.

We then started the walk back, getting a snack to keep my energy up, and visited Frog Street. It's a paved little shopping street with various stores selling second-hand goods, crafts, food and frog-themed merchandise. A little bit of everything. There was a girl with pigeon feed who was getting swarmed. Personally I don't like the idea of pigeons all over me. I got one of the fish-shaped pastries filled with ham and cheese while Amy got a custard one.

<img src="/images/matsumoto/frog-street-samurai-frog.jpg" alt="A large statue on Frog Street of a green samurai frog brandishing a sword while riding on the back of a giant brown toad with its tongue out, surrounded by a chain barrier" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

When we checked in it turned out I had booked a smoking room through ikyu.com without realising, but luckily they had one non-smoking room left which saved us getting the smell into our clothes. I'll be careful not to make that mistake again, I had kind of forgotten that hotels still allow smoking at all.

With that sorted we went out to find dinner. We ended up at Best Kitchen, which serves curry with rice plus toppings. I went with hayashi rice plus beef cubes. The sauce was really savoury and oniony without the usual curry powder, but the beef was a bit chewy and there wasn't much. Dinner cost ¥3580 (~$32 AUD), but I left a bit hungry so had to get some extra snacks on the walk home.

## Day 3: Inside the castle, soba and doburoku

The next day was another rainy day. We went back to the same bakery for breakfast since there really aren't many options. ¥1615 (~$15 AUD) got us four pastries, a bagel sandwich and a coffee.

From there we went to Matsumoto Castle. Unlike yesterday, there was no line to get inside. We paid ¥1300 (~$12 AUD) each for a ticket, though it seems like the online option saves ¥100 (~$1 AUD) and gives you a specific entry time. Unlike Himeji Castle, which is mostly left empty, this one has a number of museum exhibits, mostly showcasing firearms from the period. I prefer the open style since it better showcases the scale of the internal architecture, but the exhibits are also a nod to a petition raised to convert the castle into an exhibition space instead of it being demolished in the 19th century.

There are six floors with some very steep stairs in between and good views from the top levels. You take your shoes off to walk through the castle on the wooden floors which get quite cold by the top floor with the wind blowing through the open windows. It took about an hour to walk through the castle.

For lunch we went to Nomugi. We arrived five minutes before it opened and ended up lining up for an hour in the rain. They only have one dish, which is cold soba noodles that you dip in a sauce. Once the noodles are done you add soba-yu, the water from cooking the soba, into the sauce and drink it. It was only ¥2400 (~$22 AUD) for lunch, but for me noodles are the side for something else. Just noodles by themselves don't excite me.

<img src="/images/matsumoto/nomugi-soba.jpg" alt="Two woven bamboo baskets of cold soba noodles at Nomugi on a dark wooden table, with cups of dipping sauce, a ceramic flask of soba-yu, cups of tea and small plates of pickles and spring onion" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We had planned to go to a winery, but it was closing at 2:30 so we wouldn't have had time to get there after the lunch wait. Instead we went to Matsumoto Doburoku Brewery. Doburoku is similar to sake but lower in alcohol and unfiltered. It tastes like wine mixed with sake. Really tangy and thick. Very easy drinking, only 5% alcohol though. Amy got a flight of fruit-flavoured versions and I also got three sake to taste. The place was a bit pricey at ¥4780 (~$43 AUD), but it was good quality.

For dinner we went to Three P Noodles, which connects to our hotel, to have soupless tantan noodles which is the only thing they serve, but you can choose the side sauce. These noodles come with a bunch of toppings, including three types of mushrooms. I much preferred this to the plain soba from lunch. It was ¥2600 (~$23 AUD) for both of us, which was a pretty good deal.

## Day 4: Kamikōchi

In the morning we went for a buffet breakfast at Gusto Cafe to have fuel for the upcoming hike. They had a good mix of options, including pancakes, karaage chicken, miso katsu, salad and fruit. The quality was decent but nothing special. I ate plenty, so it was worth the ¥1500 (~$14 AUD) each.

On the way to the station we came across a demonstration for Culture Day (a national holiday), which I would describe as calligraphy cheerleading. The girls had pom poms and ink brushes and created a poster while dancing, which they then stood up to present to the crowd.

<img src="/images/matsumoto/calligraphy-performance.jpg" alt="A calligraphy club holding up a huge finished banner of bold black Japanese characters on a pale blue and yellow painted background in front of a crowd at an outdoor stage on Culture Day" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We then started the journey to Kamikōchi with a train to Shin-Shimashima followed by a bus. The bus goes through winding mountains and tunnels, and there is some beautiful scenery just on the ride. You could even see some spots where the water from the hot springs was steaming as it fell towards the river. The journey took 1 hour 45 minutes to get to our stop at Taisho Pond. Taisho Pond had lovely blue waters and overlooked the mountains though the snowy peaks were obscured by clouds at the time. The paths leading from the pond were all quite busy and we had to dodge people to keep moving forwards. There were some monkeys along the way which seemed completely oblivious to the people taking photos of them.

From there we walked to Kappabashi Bridge but didn't stop long as it was lunch time and heavily crowded with lines at all of the restaurants. We then continued on to Myojin Bridge which is a similar style but much less crowded, or maybe it was just later in the day and the crowds were thinning.

On the walk back to Kappabashi Bridge and the bus stop we saw some monkeys by the river and then a few of them swam across braving the rapids. For a second it looked like they might get swept downstream, but I think they knew what they were doing.

We finished the walk with extra time before our bus so I grabbed a beer and went back to get another photo by the bridge. By then the crowds had really died down and the clouds had parted which gave me the best view of the day.

<img src="/images/matsumoto/kamikochi-river-beer.jpg" alt="Holding up a can of Asahi Super Dry in front of the pale blue Azusa River at Kamikōchi, with Kappabashi suspension bridge, riverside lodges and cloud-covered snow-capped mountains in the background" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

It's a very well-maintained and flat course where we only gained 100m of elevation over the 11km walk, which explains the large number of people.

For dinner we went to Matsumoto Tsunagu Yokocho, which is a building full of izakaya restaurants. We picked one that specialised in gyoza and got four different types and some beef tongue. You have to order a drink and I couldn't go past the extra-large beer. Dinner came to ¥5400 (~$49 AUD), but it was a tasty meal.

## Day 5: A river run and the train to Nagano

I hadn't found time for a run yet, so on our last morning I headed out early and did an 8km run. I almost stopped early, but then I found a running track along the river with great views, which I was able to take almost all the way back into town on a nice downhill slope.

We didn't notice the gap in trains, so we had to wait until 11:30, but we sat on the top floor of the shopping mall, which has a great view over the mountains. From there we got a local train to Nagano, which also gave some great views through the mountains as we descended. It makes a funny stop at one station where it needs to reverse and switch tracks before the final leg to Nagano.

Overall Matsumoto is a bit of a sleepy mountain town even though it looks large on the map. Most shops are only open for limited hours, either only doing lunch of dinner. The castle is the main attraction in town and it made a good base to get out to Kamikōchi but I did notice many hotels more towards the mountain if one wanted to focus just on hikes.
