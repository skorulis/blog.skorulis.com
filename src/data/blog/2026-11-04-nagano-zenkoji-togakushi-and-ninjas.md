## Day 1: Zenkō-ji, a free zoo and an empty shopping street

We arrived in Nagano on Wednesday the 4th of November. It was around 1:30pm and the whole place seemed empty. Most of the shops near our hotel were closed. The only area where we consistently found things open was around the Zenkō-ji temple.

The temple turned out to be one of the nicest I've seen. There are two large, ornate wooden gates with the Nakamise-dōri shopping street between them selling local foods. The temple itself is also built from heavy, aged wood and the inside has large gold ornaments. The gardens around the temple are very neat and well maintained. The museum building looked interesting, but we didn't go inside to see the underground passage. 

<img src="/images/nagano/zenkoji-nakamise-street.jpg" alt="The stone-paved Nakamise shopping street leading up to the large wooden gate of Zenkō-ji temple, lined with traditional shops, red paper lanterns and a scattering of visitors under a cloudy sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Behind the temple are a few parks, with the top one containing Jōyama Zoo which is free to enter. They had some interesting animals, including monkeys, flamingos, penguins and sea lions. The place felt a bit run down and some of the enclosures seemed small, especially for the sea lions, though they might have just been excited because it was close to feeding time.

<img src="/images/nagano/jozan-zoo-sea-lion.jpg" alt="A sea lion raising its head out of a small bright blue pool in a fenced concrete enclosure at the free zoo behind Zenkō-ji, with a carousel visible in the background" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We walked back through some very cute and empty back streets to check in. We stayed at the Unicorn Hotel, which we picked because every room looked different. Ours had flower and bird wallpaper, a giant blue bathtub and two couches. It's all a bit dated, but clean and with a lot of space.

We headed out in search of food through Gondo Shotengai, and it continued to amaze me how empty everything felt. We decided on Orion Ramen and I got a bowl of tonkotsu ramen with thick noodles, plus rice and gyoza. The soup was very rich and salty, but I felt the noodles were a bit chewy. Overall a very good dinner for ¥3210 (~$29 AUD).

## Day 2: The Togakushi five shrines and the ninja house

In the morning we caught the bus up into the mountains to do the Togakushi five shrines pilgrimage walk. The bus climbs along looping roads through tree-covered mountains that were just changing colour for autumn. There is one spot with a great vista of the mountains through the clouds, but mostly the road is lined with trees.

The first stop at Hōkōsha took us up 291 steps straight through a forest of giant cedar trees. I found the stairs more interesting than the shrine at the top.

<img src="/images/nagano/togakushi-hokosha-steps.jpg" alt="The long, steep stone staircase up to Hōkōsha shrine at Togakushi, flanked by towering cedar trees and stone lanterns, with autumn leaves scattered on the steps and a few people climbing" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We then walked through the forest to the second and third shrines. The second was smaller, and the third had scaffolding covering the front, but it did have a small waterfall behind it and three giant cedars merged into one. There's a small town around the third shrine, so it makes a good spot to stop for lunch. The only options open were soba restaurants, one of which already had a large crowd waiting.

Instead we went to Hagakure Soba. We got in just as it opened, before we had to line up, and by the time we left there was quite a queue. They even had a soba wheat ale to try, which was quite good. I chose hot soba, which came as soba noodles in a soup. The soba had a good texture but tasted a little bland. I think the idea is to showcase the noodles, but for me noodles work better as a base for other flavours. The vegetable tempura that came with it was delicious. Lunch was ¥4700 (~$43 AUD), which was ok considering the limited options.

From there we started walking to the ninja museum, which is on the way to the final two shrines. It cost ¥700 (~$6 AUD) each to enter, plus an additional ¥200 (~$2 AUD) for the shuriken throwing. This might be the best thing on the trip so far. The museum section isn't that exciting, but the ninja house does a really good job of turning a house into a maze, with each door opened in a different way and multiple dead ends and loops. It looks small from the outside but packs in so many rooms. For a while we and a few other groups were stuck and couldn't figure out how to get out of one section. There is also a ropes course and some balancing courses. I earned myself a fan for hitting 7/7 targets in the shuriken throwing, which demonstrated my ninja skills.

<img src="/images/nagano/togakushi-ninja-training.jpg" alt="An outdoor ninja training area in the forest at Togakushi with a long blue mural of mountains and a ninja silhouette, a wobbly wooden balance beam on springs and a ground covered in fallen leaves" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

From there we walked to the final two shrines. This section is the most famous and has the most people. The uphill walk lined by cedar trees is really beautiful, and the gate with its fern-covered roof fits in perfectly. The shrines at the top are a bit meh compared to others though. The whole walk was about 11km with 420m of elevation gain, it's moderately difficult but the many stops makes it easier. We caught the bus back to Nagano from the town.

## Day 3: Obuse, a quiet run and an even quieter Friday night

The next morning we got the train to Obuse, a little town north of Nagano. We got breakfast at Obuse Iwasaki Bakery, which was absolutely tiny but did some good buns filled with red bean paste.

Obuse has an open garden system where locals allow visitors to walk through their backyards. There's a map which marks them out but we only came across 2 while wandering around. It feels odd to just walk into someone's backyard, but it's nice to see how people maintain their gardens. 

<img src="/images/nagano/obuse-open-garden.jpg" alt="A private open garden in Obuse with a large moss-covered rock, a shrub with red autumn leaves, a small stone lantern and carefully pruned pine trees against a bright blue sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We found Irohado Obuseten, a cafe selling oyaki dumplings, which are lightly fried pastries stuffed with various fillings. We tried pumpkin, mixed vegetable, mushroom and spring onion for ¥1160 (~$11 AUD). They make a nice little snack, and I think the mixed vegetable was the best filling. Amy also went to a shop to get a Mont Blanc cake, which has chestnut cream on top. I wasn't the biggest fan of the taste.

We walked to Ganshō-in temple, which took us past multiple apple orchards and vineyards. The vineyards have tall fans to prevent frost in the winter. The temple sits right below the forested mountain, which makes for a nice backdrop, but the exterior is less impressive than others we've seen.

Obuse is a cute little town, but it's more suited to oldies popping in to grab a chestnut treat than to me. Since there wasn't anything else we wanted to do, we caught the train back to Nagano and went looking for lunch. We first tried a container park to get a burger. The place was completely empty but we couldn't get served, so we went to Isomaru Suisan instead for a sashimi and grilled salmon lunch costing ¥2950 (~$27 AUD).

I had a little bit of time in the afternoon to go for a run, but despite the city being so quiet I couldn't find any nice routes that avoided traffic and lights, so I only made it 4.5km.

It was Friday afternoon, so we went searching for a bit of nightlife. We started at Shinjidai Naganoekimaeten, a cheap izakaya with a happy hour, and got chicken skin skewers along with six drinks for ¥2371 (~$22 AUD).

We then moved on to Yamanoma Brewery, where I had a nice IPA and an interesting beet saison. The owners were really friendly and there were another five people in there, but the atmosphere felt like I needed to be whispering. It didn't have the energy of a place for drinks.

Next was Minamikaze Brewery, where I didn't like the beers as much and we were the only customers. We thought about finding somewhere else, but everywhere in town looked the same: empty bars with people sitting quietly having a drink. It was all pretty depressing.

Overall, despite the ninja museum being so much fun, the actual city of Nagano was my least favourite place in Japan. There's just no energy anywhere in the city outside of the tourists wandering around the temple. It's the first time I've found it hard to find places to eat because so many places were closed.
