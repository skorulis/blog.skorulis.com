## Day 1: Fetish Club and ¥300 drink tickets in Ginza

We arrived in Tokyo on Saturday the 7th of November via the Shinkansen from Nagano. We timed it so we could check straight into our place near Ōmorimachi Station. It's a good-sized place with a separate lounge and bedroom, so we can spread out. After getting ourselves sorted we headed towards the centre of Tokyo for some drinks. The first stop was Fetish Club, which is really just a brewery. The beers were pricey but quite good. I was hoping to play the arcade games, but that wasn't an option. It ended up being ¥4092 (~$37 AUD) for 3 beers.

The next stop was Ginza 300, a standing bar where you buy drink tickets for ¥300 (~$3 AUD) each. I was having the big beers, which were 2 tickets and good value, while Amy was having gin and tonics for 1 ticket. The mixture of cheap drinks, pumping tunes and no seats meant people started mingling, and we ended up making friends with both locals and tourists. One really nice feature was the closed smoking booth at the back of the bar, which kept the rest of the bar smoke-free. I would have liked to stay longer, but we had to get the last train home.

## Day 2: Parks, a flea market, yuzu ramen and Akihabara

In the morning we went for a walk to Ōmori Furusato-no-Hamabe Riverside Park and then on to Heiwajima Park. These parks had so many different sports being played, and we came across the Ota Fureai Festa that made it quite busy. Lots of people were in traditional matching clothes and shoes. From there we kept walking north until we hit the Tokyo City Flea Market, which was in the racecourse car park and had all manner of items, though mostly second-hand clothes. It was both much larger and much busier than I expected, but I didn't see anything that caught my eye enough to buy it.

We then caught the Monorail to Takanawa Gateway and got ramen at AFURI. It's a cool little food court area where you can order ramen, curry or craft beer. I got the spicy yuzu ramen, which gave a nice citrus hit to the broth. ¥2980 (~$27 AUD) for 2 bowls of ramen.

From there we went to see the Imperial Palace gardens, which we missed last time we were in Tokyo. It's very well landscaped but not very exciting. Just lawn with sculpted black pine trees and asphalt paths to walk around. It's a box to tick, but there's not much to see.

<img src="/images/tokyo/imperial-palace-pines.jpg" alt="Rows of sculpted black pine trees on a bright green lawn at the Imperial Palace gardens, with people resting on the kerb in the sun and glass office towers rising behind under a blue sky" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Next stop was Akihabara Electric Town, which I wanted to see again. Because it was Sunday the street was blocked off to traffic, which made it nicer to walk down without everyone being crammed onto the sidewalk, but it opened back up to cars at 5pm. I saw a place called Sando Lab doing a triple egg sandwich that looked great, so I grabbed one. It was really tasty, with perfectly cooked eggs. From there we called it a night and grabbed a supermarket dinner.

## Day 3: A rainy Shibuya whisky crawl that became an all-nighter

The next morning was a bit rainy and miserable. I decided to head to the Shibuya area and have a wander. I started at Shibuya Scramble Square shopping centre hoping to get a good view of the area below, but anywhere that might have had a view was blocked off to push people into the paid options. I crossed the scramble quite a few times myself just to get from A to B, and went past the Hachikō dog statue. Even in the rain there was a huge queue to get a photo with the dog. It's very impressive how organised people were, forming a line rather than crowding around.

I went to Tower Records since it sounded interesting. It's really cool how each level has a different genre and a different feel to it. When I got to the vinyl level I also found that they had their own craft beer bar, which was just opening, so I grabbed a beer while watching everyone else sort through records.

<img src="/images/tokyo/tower-records-beer.jpg" alt="A pint glass branded Tower Records Beer filled with a hazy golden ale, sitting on a craft beer menu listing Belgian wit and hazy IPAs priced from ¥900 to ¥1600, with record store shelves blurred in the background" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Lunch was Keika Ramen, ¥1970 (~$18 AUD) with a beer. It was nice to experience sitting at a counter by myself and enjoying a bowl. I picked the special because it looked loaded. Some elements were average, but the pork was good and the pork belly was amazing. If I could just get a plate of that I would. It's also interesting to see how each place I have been does their broth in a different way. This one focused on charred spring onions.

By now it seemed like time for another beer, but most places were not open yet, so I ended up at Beer Boy in the basement of Parco. All the restaurants were busy, but not many people were grabbing a beer. I had 2 there and then moved on, and ended up walking into Whisky Bank just to see what they had. I got talking to an Australian, so tasted a Hibiki Masters Select and a Nikka Yoichi, and then had another with a Japanese couple. It was surprisingly fun for an upmarket whisky shop on a Monday afternoon. I also had some samples at Liquor Mountain up the road, but there wasn't any camaraderie there.

<img src="/images/tokyo/shibuya-whisky-tasting.jpg" alt="Me sipping a whisky sample from a small glass in front of shelves of Islay single malts including Laphroaig, Ardbeg, Lagavulin and Bowmore at a Shibuya liquor shop" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The next brewery I wanted to visit was closed, so I went to Mikkeller. I had an amazing aged whisky stout for ¥1600 (~$15 AUD), but overall everything was a bit pricey.

For dinner we went to Uobei Shibuya, which is a sushi train. Not one of the rotating belts, but the kind where your order whizzes out after you place it via a touch pad. We went through 10 plates each, mostly good, but some where I tried to be adventurous weren't to my taste. My total came to ¥1880 (~$17 AUD) and I was really full.

There was a Nikka single malt sherry and sweet variant that I really wanted to try before buying, so we went to the official Nikka whisky bar in the hope they would have some to sample. This bar ended up being more about high-end cocktails than the actual bottles of whisky, and they had less range than I had seen in other random bars. I got a flight of 3 options and Amy got an apple wine cocktail. It was a bit overpriced at ¥4800 (~$44 AUD), and I was kind of irritated that I couldn't find what I was after.

Amy went home at this point and I continued on to a standing bar called Tasuichi, just to see what it would be like. I figured I liked the one in Ginza, so I should give this a go. The big bonus was that they had a litre of beer for only ¥1000 (~$9 AUD), which I stuck to while I was there. The downside was that it was a full smoking bar, which got worse the more people arrived. I was by myself, so I started up a conversation with someone at the next table over. As the bar filled up our table gained new people to talk with. Eventually the table picked up the one person I didn't want to speak to, who kept talking about how he was going to fight this and that person over some comment he didn't like. At some point he started this with a group, who I managed to get to leave by explaining that he had been like this all night. By this point the trains had stopped, so I was all in on drinking until they started up again.

That group and I then headed to Atom, which is a multi-level DJ club. Not usually my scene, but it was fun to watch for a while. My new friends seemed to get bored pretty quickly, though, and I didn't want to leave too soon since I had already paid the cover charge. When I did want to leave I just could not figure out where the exit was. I thought this was due to my level of drunkenness, but when I ran into another group of people I had met earlier they were having the exact same issue. Eventually we did get out, and by this time I was pretty tired and ready to go, but it was still a few hours until the trains started.

I went to a nearby 7-Eleven and met a few people who were also stuck waiting for the train, and grabbed another beer out the front. It was good to hang around and have a chat, but I was feeling so tired. At some point I ran into the group from Atom again and we headed to another bar. They had a little drinking game in the back that we played as a group, which thankfully didn't involve needing to drink that often. Then it was close enough to when the trains would start, so I headed to the station and went home to sleep. I probably should have had an energy drink somewhere through the night so I didn't spend so much of it being ready for bed. But overall it felt worth it to really dive into late-night Tokyo rather than cutting it short to catch the train.

## Day 4: One last bottle of whisky and the Roppongi Hills lights

The next day was our last full day in Tokyo, and in Japan, but I slept in to catch up while Amy went shopping. I met her in Ginza to buy 1 last bottle of whisky, the one I wanted to try the night before. I figured I would just take the risk. We got some lunch near Ginza Station and then went to Roppongi Hills to see the Christmas lights. We got there just on sunset, which looked absolutely beautiful, and then went to the main viewing area. It was absolutely packed with people waiting for the perfect photo of the lights and Tokyo Tower, and people did not want to move even after getting their shot. We also went down to the street where the guards periodically allow everyone to cross and take photos, then quickly get everyone back on the sidewalk to let cars through. It's quite a pretty sight, so I can see why it was a constant rush of people. It was a nice way to finish the trip.

<img src="/images/tokyo/roppongi-sunset.jpg" alt="A vivid orange and pink sunset over Roppongi framed by glass office towers, with an elevated expressway, traffic lights and pedestrians crossing the street below" class="m-0 w-full border border-border" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

We then went back home, got some takeaway sashimi for dinner and started getting everything packed and ready for the flight the next day.

Overall, this time in Tokyo was less about the sights and more about just enjoying it as a city, since we had been there before. That's how I prefer to travel anyway. It becomes more about the people you meet than the sights I could just look up on Google.
